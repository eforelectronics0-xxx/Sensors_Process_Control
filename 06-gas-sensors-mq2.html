Topic 6 · Gas SensorsMQ-2 Gas / Smoke Sensor

1Introduction

A gas sensor detects the presence or concentration of a gas in the air. The most popular and cheapest family for student projects and domestic alarms is the MQ series of semiconductor (MOS) gas sensors — MQ-2 (smoke, LPG, propane), MQ-3 (alcohol), MQ-4 (methane, CNG), MQ-6 (LPG), MQ-7 (carbon monoxide), MQ-135 (air quality, NH₃, CO₂). In this topic we study the MQ-2 in detail.

2Construction of MQ-2

Inside the steel mesh there is a small alumina (Al₂O₃) ceramic tube. On the outer surface of this tube a layer of tin dioxide is deposited; this is the sensing layer. A heating coil is placed inside the tube to keep the sensing layer at a high temperature, because the chemical reaction takes place only when it is hot. Two platinum electrodes collect the current from the sensing layer. The heater and the sensor are electrically separate, so the MQ-2 has six pins — two heater pins (H) and four sensing pins (A and B, internally connected in pairs).

3Working

When the sensor is heated in clean air, oxygen molecules from the air get adsorbed on the surface of SnO₂ and capture free electrons to form O⁻ and O₂⁻ ions. Because the electrons are trapped, the resistance of the sensing layer is high (about 10 kΩ or more, this is called R₀).

When a reducing gas such as LPG, smoke or propane comes in contact with the hot surface, it reacts with the adsorbed oxygen ions. The trapped electrons are released back into the tin dioxide, so the number of free electrons increases and the resistance falls. The higher the gas concentration, the lower the resistance. In a potential divider circuit this fall of resistance gives a rise in output voltage, which the microcontroller reads through the ADC. If the voltage crosses the set limit, the controller switches ON the buzzer, the exhaust fan or the relay.

4Applications

  • LPG leakage alarm in domestic kitchens and cylinder stores.
  • Smoke detector combined with fire alarm systems.
  • Air quality monitoring in offices, laboratories and smart homes.
  • Portable gas leak detector for pipeline checking.
  • Automated exhaust fan / gas valve shut-off systems.
Care while using MQ-2 The sensor needs a pre-heat (burn-in) time of 24 to 48 hours for the first use and 2 to 3 minutes for normal use, otherwise the reading drifts. The heater makes the sensor hot, so do not touch it and do not use it in a very dusty or silicon-vapour atmosphere.

5Worked numericals

Problem 1. An MQ-2 is wired with RL = 10 kΩ across 5 V and reads Vout = 2.0 V in clean air where R0 gives Vout0 = 1.0 V. Find Rs / R0.

Solution. Divider: Vout = Vcc × RL / (Rs + RL) Clean air: 1.0 = 5 × 10 / (R0 + 10) ⇒ R0 = 40 kΩ Gas present: 2.0 = 5 × 10 / (Rs + 10) ⇒ Rs = 15 kΩ Ratio Rs/R0 = 15/40 = 0.375 — read it off the log-log curve for the gas in question. Rs/R0 = 0.375 (concentration comes from the datasheet curve).

6Viva questions and answers

Q1. What material is the sensing element of MQ-2?
A. Tin dioxide (SnO₂), an n-type semiconductor whose surface resistance changes when combustible gases adsorb on it.

Q2. Why is a heater coil inside the can?
A. The sensing reaction needs a controlled high surface temperature, roughly 200 to 400 °C, supplied by the internal nichrome heater.

Q3. Does MQ-2 consume oxygen?
A. Yes — it needs fresh air reference oxygen at the surface, so it should not be sealed in a tiny volume without ventilation.

Q4. Is the sensor output linear in gas concentration?
A. No. The relationship is log-log; the datasheet curve of Rs/R0 against ppm is used, or a microcontroller fits that curve.

Q5. How is selectivity improved in a gas sensor array?
A. Several sensors with different dopants (Sn, Pd, Al) are combined and their pattern is classified by a small algorithm.

Q6. What safety rating do LPG sensors often carry?
A. Many MQ-2 modules are advertised as explosion-proof and flameproof for domestic LPG leak alarm use, but the module still needs certified installation.

7Summary

The MQ-2 is a resistive SnO₂ gas sensor: combustible gas on the heated surface changes the semiconductor resistance, and a simple load resistor converts that change into a voltage the ADC can read. Accurate work depends on knowing R₀ at baseline, following the log-log response curve rather than assuming linearity, respecting heater temperature and burn-in, and never trapping the sensor away from fresh air.
