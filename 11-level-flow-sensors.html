1Level Sensors

Definition A level sensor measures the height of a liquid (or solid) surface inside a tank, silo or vessel and converts it into an electrical signal. Measurement may be point level (liquid present / absent at one point) or continuous level (exact height).

2Flow Sensors

Definition A flow sensor (flow meter) measures the rate of flow of a liquid or gas through a pipe. It is expressed as volumetric flow (litre per minute, m³/hr) or mass flow (kg/hr).

2.1 Orifice plate (differential pressure flow meter)

A thin plate with a small concentric hole is inserted in the pipe. The flow velocity increases at the constriction, so the pressure there falls (Bernoulli's theorem). The pressure difference P₁ − P₂ across the plate is measured with a differential pressure transmitter and the flow is calculated. This is the cheapest and most widely used industrial flow meter.

2.2 Turbine flow meter

A small multi-blade rotor is placed in the flow. The flowing fluid rotates the rotor and its speed is proportional to the flow velocity. A magnetic pickup coil counts the pulses generated by each passing blade. Very accurate for clean liquids and gases.

2.3 Electromagnetic flow meter

The pipe is non-magnetic and lined with an insulator. When a conductive liquid flows through a magnetic field, an EMF is induced across two electrodes: E = B L v. It has no moving part and no obstruction, so it is used for sewage, slurry and chemicals.

2.4 Rotameter (variable area meter)

A float is kept inside a vertical tapered glass tube. The flow lifts the float until the upward drag equals the weight of the float. The height of the float gives the flow directly. It is a simple local indicator, no power required.

Applications

  • Water supply metering, irrigation and sewage flow measurement.
  • Steam, fuel gas and air flow in boilers and furnaces.
  • Chemical dosing, batching and custody transfer of petroleum.
  • Medical — oxygen flow meter in anaesthesia machines.
One line summary Level tells "how much height of material is inside the tank"; flow tells "how much quantity is passing through the pipe every second". Both are needed to control a tank — inflow, outflow and level must always be balanced.

3Worked numericals

Problem 1. An orifice plate meter is calibrated so that Q = 0.05 × √ΔP with ΔP in Pa and Q in m³/h. Find Q at ΔP = 400 Pa, and the new ΔP if Q doubles.

Solution. Q = 0.05 × √400 = 0.05 × 20 = 1.0 m³/h. Q ∝ √ΔP, so doubling Q needs 4× ΔP. New ΔP = 400 × 4 = 1600 Pa. Q = 1.0 m³/h at 400 Pa; doubled flow needs 1600 Pa.

4Viva questions and answers

Q1. Why is Q proportional to the square root of ΔP in an orifice meter?
A. Bernoulli's equation gives velocity ∝ √ΔP and volumetric flow is velocity times area, so the differential pressure must be square-rooted in the transmitter.

Q2. What fluids can an electromagnetic flow meter handle?
A. Any liquid that conducts at least about 5 µS/cm — water, acids, slurries — but not pure solvents or oil.

Q3. How does a guided radar (TDR) level probe work?
A. A pulse travels down the probe, reflects at the liquid surface, and the time difference gives level regardless of foam or density changes.

Q4. Why is a bypass rotameter not suitable for billing?
A. It is a variable-area local indicator with modest accuracy; fiscal transfer needs certified meters with traceable calibration.

Q5. What is totaliser functionality in a flow transmitter?
A. It integrates pulse or flow-rate input over time to display total volume or mass, like an odometer for fluid.

Q6. What causes a float switch to stick?
A. Scale, wax, sticky residues or mechanical wear; materials must be chosen compatible with the process fluid.

5Summary

Level instrumentation determines how much is in the tank, flow instrumentation determines how fast it moves, and both feed inventory control and billing. Ultrasonic, radar, float and differential-pressure methods cover level; orifice, turbine, electromagnetic and variable-area methods cover flow. Every selection must match fluid properties and required accuracy, and every meter needs periodic verification against a known volume.
