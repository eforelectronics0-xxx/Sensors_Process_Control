Topic 3 · Stress, Strain and Load CellsStrain Gauge, Wheatstone Bridge, Load Cell

1Stress, strain and the basic idea

When a force is applied on a body, the internal resisting force per unit area is called stress and the deformation produced (change in length per original length) is called strain. Strain is a ratio of two lengths, so it has no unit and is expressed in microstrain (µε).

σ = F / A   (N/m² = Pa)      ε = ΔL / L      σ = E × ε  (Hooke's law)

Here E is the modulus of elasticity (Young's modulus) of the material. The sensor which measures strain is the strain gauge, and a strain gauge mounted on a properly shaped metal element becomes a load cell.

2Strain Gauge — Construction

A strain gauge consists of a very thin metallic foil or fine wire (constantan, nichrome or karma alloy) arranged in a zig-zag grid pattern and bonded between two thin insulating backing sheets. The grid is bonded to the test surface with a special adhesive (epoxy or cyanoacrylate). Two copper lead wires are soldered to the ends of the grid. The whole gauge is only a few millimetres square and its nominal resistance is 120 Ω, 350 Ω or 1000 Ω.

3Working of the strain gauge

When the surface stretches, the grid also stretches. Its length increases and its cross-section decreases, so the resistance increases. When the surface is compressed, the resistance decreases. The sensitivity is expressed by the gauge factor:

G = (ΔR / R) / ε    (about 2 for metal foil gauges)

The problem is that the change is very small. For example a 120 Ω gauge at 1000 µε changes only by 0.24 Ω. Such a tiny change can never be measured with an ordinary multimeter, so the gauge is always connected in a Wheatstone bridge.

4Load Cell — Construction and Working

A load cell is a complete transducer assembly in which four strain gauges are bonded on a properly designed elastic metal element (alloy steel or aluminium) called the flexure / proof body. Common shapes are cantilever beam (bending type, used in platform weighing), S-type / tension-compression, shear beam and column / pancake type (used for very large loads).

The four gauges are connected in a full Wheatstone bridge. When load is applied, two gauges are in tension (R increases) and two are in compression (R decreases). This gives four times more output than a single gauge and also cancels temperature error. The output is a few millivolts per volt of excitation (for example 2 mV/V) which is amplified by an HX711 / instrumentation amplifier and then converted to digital by the ADC.

Applications of load cells

  • Electronic weighing machines — kitchen scale, platform scale, weigh bridge for trucks.
  • Hopper and silo weighing in cement, sugar and food industries.
  • Force measurement in universal testing machines (tensile / compression testing).
  • Crane overload protection, torque wrenches, dynamometers.
  • Medical bed weighing, baggage weighing at airports.
Important points for viva (i) Strain gauge is a passive transducer. (ii) Gauge factor G ≈ 2. (iii) Bridge output is millivolts, so amplification is compulsory. (iv) Four-gauge (full bridge) arrangement gives maximum output and automatic temperature compensation. (v) Load cell is a secondary transducer because it converts force → strain → resistance → voltage.

5Worked numericals

Problem 1. A strain gauge of 120 Ω with gauge factor GF = 2.0 is bonded to a beam. The strain is 500 µε (500×10−6). Find the change in resistance and the bridge output if the supply is 5 V.

Solution. ΔR/R = GF × ε = 2.0 × 500×10−6 = 1.0×10−3 ΔR = 120 × 10−3 = 0.12 Ω For a quarter bridge, Vout ≈ (Vs/4) × ΔR/R = 5/4 × 0.001 = 1.25 mV ΔR = 0.12 Ω; bridge output ≈ 1.25 mV.

6Viva questions and answers

Q1. Define gauge factor.
A. GF = (ΔR/R) / ε, the fractional resistance change per unit strain; metal gauges are near 2, semiconductor gauges can exceed 100.

Q2. Why is a Wheatstone bridge used with strain gauges?
A. The resistance change is tiny; the bridge converts it into a differential voltage and cancels common effects such as temperature drift.

Q3. What does a quarter, half and full bridge mean?
A. One, two or four active gauges in the bridge arms; more active arms give more sensitivity and better temperature compensation.

Q4. How does a load cell actually work?
A. Applied force strains a calibrated elastic body; bonded gauges read that strain, which is proportional to force within the elastic limit.

Q5. Why do we need four load cells on a tank?
A. To support each leg, sum the weights, and remain accurate when the centre of gravity shifts off centre.

7Summary

Stress and strain describe how a material deforms under load, a strain gauge turns that deformation into a fractional resistance change through the gauge factor, and a bridge plus amplifier lifts the millivolt signal to a usable level. Load cells package the same principle into calibrated force bodies used in weighing systems.
