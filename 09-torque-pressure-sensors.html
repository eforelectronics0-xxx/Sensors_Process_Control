1Torque Sensors

Definition Torque is the turning (twisting) effect of a force applied at some distance from the axis of rotation. Torque T = F × r (N-m). A torque sensor measures this twisting action on a rotating shaft.

1.1 Construction and working (strain gauge torque sensor)

When a shaft transmits power it twists slightly. This twist produces shear stress on the surface of the shaft. The maximum shear stress occurs on planes inclined at 45° to the shaft axis. Therefore four strain gauges are bonded on the shaft at ±45° and connected as a full Wheatstone bridge — two in tension and two in compression. When the shaft twists, the bridge unbalances and gives an output voltage proportional to the applied torque.

Because the shaft is rotating, the small bridge signal must be transferred to the stationary side. This is done by slip rings and brushes, by rotary transformers or, in modern sensors, by telemetry (wireless) or magneto-elastic coupling.

Other torque sensors

  • Magneto-elastic / magnetostrictive — twist changes the magnetic permeability of the shaft; contactless.
  • Optical torque sensor — two encoder discs on the shaft, twist changes the phase between the two pulse trains.
  • Reaction torque sensor — measures the torque on the stationary housing of a motor (used in dynamometers).

Applications of torque sensors

  • Engine and motor dynamometers, brake testing.
  • Power measurement of pumps, compressors, gear boxes, turbines.
  • Electric power steering and tightening tools (torque wrench).
  • Conveyor and crane drive monitoring.

2Pressure Sensors

Definition Pressure is the force acting per unit area, P = F/A, measured in Pa, bar, psi or mm of Hg. A pressure sensor converts this pressure into an electrical signal, usually by first converting it into a deflection of an elastic element.

2.1 Bourdon tube pressure gauge

A C-shaped (or spiral) hollow tube of elliptical cross-section is used. When pressure is applied inside the tube it tends to become circular, so the free end of the C straightens out. This movement is amplified by a link and pinion and moves a pointer over a calibrated dial. It is a purely mechanical device, very rugged and cheap.

2.2 Strain gauge / diaphragm pressure sensor

Pressure is applied on one side of a thin stainless steel diaphragm. Four strain gauges bonded on the diaphragm form a Wheatstone bridge. The deflection is proportional to pressure, so the bridge output (in mV) is proportional to pressure. Such sensors are used inside pressure transmitters which give a 4–20 mA output.

2.3 Piezoresistive pressure sensor

Here a silicon diaphragm is micro-machined and piezoresistors are diffused into it. The resistance of silicon changes directly with stress, so no bonding is needed. Very small, cheap and used in mobile phones, medical devices and car MAP sensors.

Applications of pressure sensors

  • Boiler, steam line, hydraulic and pneumatic system pressure monitoring.
  • Blood pressure measurement (medical), tyre pressure monitoring (TPMS).
  • Level measurement by hydrostatic head (P = ρgh).
  • Flow measurement using differential pressure across an orifice.
  • Altitude measurement in aircraft (barometric pressure).

3Worked numericals

Problem 1. A diaphragm pressure sensor of area 2 cm² sees 15 bar gauge. Find the force on the diaphragm (1 bar = 105 Pa).

Solution. A = 2 cm² = 2 × 10−4 m². P = 15 bar = 1.5 × 106 Pa. F = P × A = 1.5×106 × 2×10−4 = 300 N. F = 300 N on the diaphragm.

4Viva questions and answers

Q1. Distinguish gauge, absolute and differential pressure.
A. Gauge is referenced to atmosphere, absolute to vacuum, differential is the difference between two process points (e.g. across an orifice).

Q2. How does a Bourdon tube work?
A. A curved hollow tube tends to straighten as internal pressure rises; the tip motion drives a gear and pointer.

Q3. What is overpressure rating?
A. The maximum pressure a sensor survives without calibration shift, often 2–3 times full scale.

Q4. Why do pressure transmitters show a small static zero error?
A. Sealed systems, temperature effects on fill fluid, or installing a gauge reference where absolute was specified.

Q5. What is impulse piping?
A. The tube that connects the process tap to the transmitter; it must be lagged, valved and free of trapped gas or liquid that would distort the reading.

Q6. Where are piezoresistive sensors commonly used?
A. Manifold absolute-pressure (MAP) sensors in engines, tyre-pressure monitors and medical devices — where size and cost dominate.

5Summary

Pressure sensors all measure the force of a fluid on a defined area, but they differ in the elastic element used — Bourdon tube, corrugated diaphragm or silicon bridge — and in the electrical readout. Range, reference (gauge/absolute/differential), overpressure margin and 4–20 mA scaling are the four decisions that determine whether a pressure installation measures correctly on day one and keeps measuring after water hammer and blockage events.
