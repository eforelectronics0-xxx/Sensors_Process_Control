Topic 2 · Temperature SensorsThermistor and LM35

1Introduction to temperature measurement

Temperature is the most commonly measured quantity in any process industry — boilers, distillation columns, plastic moulding, food processing, incubators and even our mobile phone battery. The sensors used are broadly of two families: contact type (thermocouple, RTD, thermistor, IC sensor) which touch the body, and non-contact type (infrared pyrometer) which measures the radiated heat. In this topic we study the two most important ones for our syllabus: the Thermistor and the LM35.

2Thermistor (Thermally Sensitive Resistor)

2.1 Construction

A thermistor is made by sintering (baking at high temperature) a mixture of semiconducting metal oxides such as manganese, nickel, cobalt, copper and iron oxides with a binder. The baked ceramic pellet is only a few millimetres in size. Two platinum or silver leads are attached to it and the whole assembly is sealed inside a glass or epoxy coating to protect it from moisture and chemicals. Because the sensing bead is very small, the thermistor responds very fast to a temperature change.

2.2 Types

  • NTC thermistor (Negative Temperature Coefficient) — resistance decreases when temperature increases. Most common type.
  • PTC thermistor (Positive Temperature Coefficient) — resistance increases when temperature increases. Used for over-current protection and motor starting.

2.3 Working

In a semiconductor the number of free charge carriers increases when it is heated. Therefore in an NTC thermistor, more heat → more free electrons → lower resistance. The relation between resistance and temperature is exponential, not linear:

RT = R0 · eβ(1/T − 1/T0)    where T is in kelvin and β is the material constant (3000 – 5000 K)

A thermistor is a passive device, so it must be connected in a voltage divider with a fixed resistor and excited from a stable DC supply. The output voltage at the junction changes with temperature and is read by the ADC of a microcontroller.

2.4 Applications of thermistor

  • Temperature measurement in battery chargers, inverters and SMPS.
  • Inrush current limiting — PTC thermistor in series with the supply.
  • Thermal protection of motors, transformers and power amplifiers.
  • Digital thermometers, incubators, green-house controllers, air-conditioners.
  • Cold-junction compensation of thermocouples and hot-wire anemometers (flow of air).

3LM35 Temperature Sensor IC

3.1 Introduction and Construction

The LM35 is a precision integrated-circuit temperature sensor made in silicon. Its output voltage is directly proportional to the Celsius temperature, so no external calibration and no mathematics of subtraction is needed. It is available in three packages: TO-92 (plastic, 3 pins — most common), TO-46 (metal can) and SOIC (surface mount). Inside the chip there is a temperature sensing junction, a buffer amplifier and a reference circuit, all on one silicon die.

Pin (TO-92, flat side towards us)NameFunction
Pin 1 (left)VCCSupply +4 V to +30 V (normally +5 V)
Pin 2 (middle)VOUTAnalog output, 10 mV per °C
Pin 3 (right)GNDGround / 0 V

3.2 Working

The forward voltage of a silicon junction falls by about 2 mV for every 1 °C rise. The LM35 uses two matched junctions and an internal amplifier to produce a very clean output of exactly 10 mV/°C. That is, at 25 °C the output is 250 mV and at 100 °C it is 1 V. The device draws only about 60 µA, so self heating is less than 0.1 °C in still air.

VOUT = 10 mV × T (°C)  →  T = VOUT / 0.010 °C

3.3 Applications of LM35

  • Digital thermometers and temperature display panels.
  • Temperature control of incubators, green houses, water heaters, CPU cooling fans.
  • Cold junction compensation and battery management systems.
  • Any student project with Arduino / 8051 because the interfacing is very simple.

4Worked numericals

Problem 1. An NTC thermistor has β = 4000 K and R = 10 kΩ at T0 = 298 K (25 °C). Find its resistance at T = 308 K (35 °C).

Solution. RT = R0 · eβ(1/T − 1/T0) 1/T − 1/T0 = 1/308 − 1/298 = 3.247×10−3 − 3.356×10−3 = −1.088×10−4 K−1 Exponent = 4000 × (−1.088×10−4) = −0.435 RT = 10 × e−0.435 = 10 × 0.647 = 6.47 kΩ R35°C ≈ 6.47 kΩ (resistance falls as temperature rises).

5Viva questions and answers

Q1. Why is an NTC called negative temperature coefficient?
A. Its resistance decreases when temperature increases, unlike a metal RTD which shows a small positive coefficient.

Q2. Give the LM35 sensitivity and supply range.
A. Sensitivity is 10 mV/°C (0 V at 0 °C); supply is +4 V to +30 V, typically +5 V.

6Summary

Temperature measurement offers three workhorse devices: the thermistor for a large, cheap, non-linear resistance change over a short range; the RTD for stable, nearly linear precision; and integrated sensors like the LM35 that deliver a ready-made 10 mV/°C voltage.
