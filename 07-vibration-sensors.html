Topic 7 · Vibration SensorsAccelerometer, Piezo and Vibration Switch

1Introduction and need

Vibration is the to-and-fro oscillatory motion of a machine part about its mean position. Every rotating machine vibrates, but when the vibration crosses a limit it means that something is wrong — imbalance, misalignment, loose bearing, bent shaft or cavitation. A vibration sensor measures this oscillation so that the fault can be detected before the machine fails. This is called condition monitoring or predictive maintenance.

Vibration is described by three quantities: displacement (micron), velocity (mm/s) and acceleration (g or m/s²), together with its frequency in Hz.

2Piezoelectric Accelerometer — Construction

It is the most common industrial vibration sensor. A seismic mass is placed on a piezoelectric crystal (quartz or PZT ceramic). The crystal is sandwiched between two electrodes and the whole assembly is sealed in a steel case. When the case vibrates, the mass presses the crystal with a varying force and the crystal generates a charge.

3Working

The piezoelectric effect states that when a mechanical force is applied on certain crystals, electric charges appear on their faces. The generated charge Q = d × F, where d is the piezoelectric constant and F is the applied force. Since the force on the mass is F = m × a, the charge produced is directly proportional to the acceleration of the vibration. This very small charge must be converted by a charge amplifier or the built-in IEPE amplifier into a usable voltage (typically 100 mV/g).

4Other vibration sensors

4.1 Vibration switch (SW-18020P / ball type)

It is a simple digital sensor. A small metal ball is kept inside a cylindrical spring or cavity. When vibration occurs the ball touches the walls and the contacts close, giving a LOW or HIGH signal. It is used where only "vibration present / absent" information is needed.

4.2 MEMS accelerometer (ADXL335 / MPU-6050)

A tiny silicon cantilever with a proof mass is made by micro-machining. Its capacitance changes with movement and the on-chip circuit converts it to an analog voltage (ADXL335) or to I²C digital data (MPU-6050). Used in mobile phones, drones, car airbags and step counting watches.

5Applications

  • Predictive maintenance of motors, pumps, fans, gear boxes, compressors.
  • Bearing fault detection and shaft imbalance analysis.
  • Earthquake and structural health monitoring of bridges and buildings.
  • Vehicle crash detection (airbag), anti-theft alarms, mobile screen rotation.
  • Washing machine unbalance detection and drone stabilisation.

6Worked numericals

Problem 1. A piezoelectric accelerometer has sensitivity 100 pC/g and is connected to a charge amplifier with Cf = 1000 pF. Find the amplifier output at 10 g.

Solution. Charge Q = 100 pC/g × 10 g = 1000 pC. Vout = Q / Cf = 1000 pC / 1000 pF = 1.0 V. Charge amplifiers are preferred because cable capacitance does not change the gain. Vout = 1.0 V.

Problem 2. An accelerometer with 500 mV/g output is sampled while the machine vibrates at 0.4 g. The controller alarms above 1.0 g. Find the ADC counts at 0.4 g with a 3.3 V, 12-bit ADC.

Solution. V = 0.5 V/g × 0.4 g = 0.2 V. Counts = 0.2 / 3.3 × 4095 = 248. Alarm level 1.0 g = 0.5 V ⇒ 620 counts — threshold can be set directly in counts. ADC code ≈ 248 at 0.4 g.

7Viva questions and answers

Q1. What does an accelerometer actually measure?
A. It measures the force needed to accelerate its own proof mass; by Newton's law that force divided by mass equals the vibration acceleration.

Q2. Why do piezo accelerometers need a charge or IEPE amplifier?
A. The crystal generates charge but almost no current; a charge amplifier or built-in IEPE conditioning converts it to a usable voltage before the cable.

Q3. What is the resonant frequency of a vibration sensor?
A. The mounting-structure natural frequency; measurements are only trusted well below it, usually below one third.

Q4. Why are MEMS accelerometers popular in modern machines?
A. They are tiny, cheap, low power and put X, Y, Z plus temperature on one digital interface straight into a microcontroller.

Q5. What is a vibration switch used for?
A. It gives a simple trip output when vibration crosses a set level — cheap protection for pumps, fans and washers without full spectrum analysis.

Q6. How does sampling rate relate to vibration analysis?
A. The sample rate must be at least twice the highest frequency of interest (Nyquist); bearing faults often need several kilohertz sampling.

8Summary

Vibration sensors convert mechanical oscillation into an electrical signal: piezoelectric accelerometers for wide bandwidth and harsh duty, MEMS devices for compact three-axis digital sensing, and simple vibration switches for alarm duty. The sensor output is a charge or voltage proportional to acceleration, the mounting must be mechanically stiff, and the electronics must resolve the tiny signals before cable noise takes over.
